F-5 — Why the local validation kept mis-ranking candidates

This is the most important finding of the session, and it is a property of the measurement rather than of any model.

Uniform random noise scores 0.1199 and beats every physically motivated detector, including the supplied magnetic edge band and a fresh tilt-angle edge operator, at both budgets. The reference field that holds 0.1563 on the public leaderboard scores 0.1253 on the same test — barely above noise.

The reason is geometrical. The metric credits a prediction anywhere within 300 m of a truth pixel, the catalogue is spread across the whole map, and a real submission emits only about 3 % of the valid area. A detector that concentrates its budget on the strongest anomaly leaves most of the map uncovered; noise covers everything evenly and collects the credit. The catalogue-recovery holdout at competition density is dominated by coverage, not by detection, which is exactly why a leave-one-fault-system-out proxy correlated −0.20 with the public leaderboard in the sibling audit.

The consequence for the next iteration is concrete: a local score computed this way cannot rank candidates, and an improvement in marginal precision must be demonstrated at the emission density the leaderboard actually uses. Anything measured only at a sparse budget is uninformative, and re-tuning parameters against it is wasted effort.

F-6 — The catalogue is more fragmented than a fault map suggests

Reading the supplied labels raster directly: 3199 8-connected components, median size 12.0 pixels, largest 360 pixels. Most mapped "faults" in this raster are 1–2 km segments. Any structural inference built on the catalogue's topology — bridging, linking, ordering — is operating on a heavily segmented version of the real network, and that must be accounted for before the result is interpreted geologically.

F-7 — The off-catalogue arm: Qfaults refused, SGMC surrogate built

The hide-and-recover holdout cannot rank a discovery hypothesis, because its truth is the catalogue. The obvious second arm — an independent official fault compilation — was attempted with the USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database (Qfaults, DOI 10.5066/P9BCVRCK) and quantitatively refused by the sibling project's cross-catalogue measurement: 60938 of its 60939 in-footprint pixels are already within the credit radius of a competition label, leaving 1 independent pixel. Inside this footprint, Qfaults and the competition catalogue are the same lines — verified from the raster bytes, not inferred. (Source: GEMSDOE/data/evidence/xcat/transfer_report.json.)

The remaining surrogate is the USGS State Geologic Map Compilation structure raster (Horton, San Juan and Stoeser, 2017, DOI 10.3133/ds1052): 59035 pixels of mapped structure lie beyond the catalogue's 300 m credit halo. The filler's marginal precision (0.0218–0.0227) is below the break-even threshold in every fold, so the union was refused publication by the gate. Together with the earlier H6 truncation curve (monotone to full support) and the family's thinned submissions losing points on the leaderboard (12GEMSDOE 0.1294 < 0.1563), this closes emission shaping from three directions. Spreading does raise raw coverage — lattice-NULL reaches 0.1499 against clustered-NULL's 0.1188 on the holdout — but that credit is already spoken for once a real support is emitted. Only detection skill moves the score.

F-9 — The supervised expression model recovers withheld faults

The one approach missing from this repository was the reference solution's: a model trained to recognise catalogue fault expression. It was built with strict leakage control — no location or distance features, training positives from visible catalogue pixels only, the blind corridor masked to NaN at training and inference — and tested on the frozen hide-and-recover protocol:

Mean dense DTI 0.2795 against the current holdout best of 0.1253 and the uniform-random baseline of 0.1199. Marginal precision clears break-even in every fold. This is the first candidate in the project's history to clear the gate, and it is published as a uniquely named download. Its one failure is on record too: on the SGMC off-catalogue screen it scores 0.1225 — below the reference support and far below random — so the model recovers catalogue-like faults but has not demonstrated discovery of structure unlike the catalogue. That is exactly the quantity the leaderboard will judge.
