Executive summary

This repository is the validation and forensic arm of the DOE GEMS Prize Challenge effort. It answers two questions that score arithmetic cannot answer, and it publishes submissions that are verified at the byte level rather than assumed to be valid.

Why the family keeps scoring 0.1563

The 0.1563 raster (sha256 7f00890a…) is published in 15 locations across 4 different repositories. Two accounts uploading that file necessarily receive the same score, because the scored object is identical. It is one result, submitted repeatedly — not several models that happened to agree.

A detector that recovers withheld faults

The supervised expression model (D) is the first candidate to clear the hide-and-recover gate: mean dense DTI 0.2795 against the current best of 0.1253. No location features: it recognises expression, not memorised geography.

The one untried channel

The 19 delivered bands contain no radiometric channel, verified band by band from the file itself. Airborne potassium maps alteration along faults that have no scarp — exactly the faults a scarp-based catalogue is missing. The data are free and official; the source and the paper that uses them this way are on the sources page.
